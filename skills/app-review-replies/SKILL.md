# App Review Replies

A reply to a store review is public. The reviewer is notified, and every future user reads it while deciding whether to download. Write for both. A short, warm, specific reply can bring a reviewer back and lift a rating. A long, defensive or generic one costs downloads.

The numbers this job moves: **reply coverage** (how many of the reviews you could answer you did answer), **time to reply**, and **ratings raised after a reply**. Behind them sits the one that matters most: the app's rating.

This skill is the method we use to reply to reviews for many apps, step by step, so you can do it by hand.

## The loop

1. **Set the app's reply settings once** (step 1).
2. **Every day**: pull new and edited reviews, pick which to answer (step 2), read each in its own language (step 3).
3. **Draft** each reply by the rules (steps 4 and 5).
4. **Check** every draft before it goes out (step 6).
5. **Approve and send**, then watch that it appears (step 8).
6. **Learn**: turn lasting feedback on drafts into house rules (step 7).
7. **Every week**: read reviews for themes, and judge the results (how to judge, below).

## 1. Decide the settings once, per app

Write these down before the first reply. Every reply follows them.

- **The sign-off.** One fixed line that ends every reply, such as "The Quillnook Team". If you don't choose one, use "The <app name> Team" (and if the name already starts with "The", just "<app name> Team", never "The The ..."). The sign-off is the team, never a made-up character or a mascot, and never one person's name.
- **The support address.** The one email address replies may name. If there is none, replies send people to support inside the app.
- **The tone.** Professional and courteous, friendly and conversational, or casual like a friend. Pick one for the app.
- **Version numbers.** Whether replies may name an app version ("fixed in 4.2.1"). Some teams prefer never to.
- **House rules.** Short lines the team agrees on, such as "never offer refunds in a reply" or "no emoji on 1-star reviews". Keep each to one sentence.
- **Who sends.** Drafts that a person approves, or replies that send on their own. Start with approval.

## 2. Pick which reviews to answer

1. **New reviews first.** We don't draft replies for reviews older than about 30 days: the reviewer has moved on, and a late reply reads as a bot. The exception is an old review about a problem that still exists.
2. **Low ratings that name a fixable problem**: a crash, a lost purchase, a confusing step.
3. **Reviews the user edited** after you replied (step 5).
4. **Questions**, where a reader is waiting for an answer.
5. **Praise that names something specific.** A short thank-you.

If you decided not to answer a review and dropped its draft, don't draft it again automatically; only when someone asks.

**Working through a backlog** (an app that has never replied, or a month away):

- Take only the last 30 days, plus older low ratings about problems that still exist.
- Sort by rating, lowest first, then newest first.
- Group by theme (all the sync complaints together). Write the first reply in a group carefully; the rest share its facts but not its wording.
- Do it in daily batches. A hundred replies in one hour read as a bot to anyone who scrolls the page.

## 3. Read the review in its own language

Work out the language from the words of the review, not from the reviewer's country. People often write in a language other than their store's default. Use the country only as a tiebreak for a very short review. Then reply in that language. If you don't read it, translate the review to understand it, write the reply in the reviewer's language, and keep a translation of your reply to check what it says.

## 4. Write the reply

**The rules every reply follows:**

- **Match the length to the review.** A short review gets a short reply. Never more than two or three short sentences a reader takes in at a glance; as a rule of thumb, 40 words at most. Google Play also caps replies at 350 characters.
- **Write one final reply.** No note about the reply, no second version, nothing before or after it.
- **Be genuine and personal, not robotic.** Answer the specific point they made ("the export to PDF failing"), so it is clearly not a template.
- **Match the rating.** Empathy and an apology where one is due for a low rating; real thanks for a high one.
- **Never be defensive or argumentative.**
- **Route problems to support with the real address only.** Name the configured support address, written exactly. Never write any other email address, link or phone number, and never guess one ("support@app.com" that nobody set up). With no address set, say to contact support in the app.
- **No em dashes.** Use a comma, a colon or a full stop.
- **App Store: never use the < or > characters.** App Store Connect rejects them. Write a menu path as "Settings → Subscription".
- **End with the sign-off, word for word**, even when the reply is in another language. The sign-off is never translated.
- **Emoji only when the tone is friendly or casual, and no house rule forbids them.** A rule that says "no emoji" always wins.
- **Never promise what isn't decided**: a date, a feature, a refund you can't give.

**For a 1-star review**, don't try to work out the cause in public. Apologise and send them to support (or, with no support address, say you are looking into it).

**Refund requests.** On the App Store only Apple can refund a purchase: point the person to Apple's own refund request page, from their purchase history. On Google Play the developer can refund from Play Console, so the reply can say you'll help through support. Never promise a refund in public.

**What each kind of review needs:**

| The review | What the reply does |
|---|---|
| A bug or a crash | Apologise, say what to do now (update, a workaround), send them to support |
| A lost purchase or a billing problem | Apologise, send them to support; on the App Store, point refunds to Apple |
| A feature request | Thank them, say it's noted; never promise it or a date |
| A price complaint | Acknowledge it; say what the price includes or what's free; never argue the price |
| Too many ads | Acknowledge it; name an ad-free option only if it exists |
| A question | Answer it in one sentence, or say where the answer is in the app |
| Praise | Thank them, and name the thing they liked |
| A comparison with another app | Thank them; talk about this app, never the other one |
| A review meant for another app | Say politely that it looks like it's about a different app |

**Two special cases:**

- The reviewer says they didn't write the review, or their account was taken over: take it seriously and point them to support.
- The review looks like spam, fraud or abuse: stay professional and keep it very short. Report it through the store console; never argue in public.

## 5. Answer a review that was edited

When someone changes a review after you replied, reply to what it says now.

- Thank them for updating it.
- If the rating went up, say thanks and name what changed for them.
- If it went down, answer the new concern with the same care as a first reply.
- If the new text contradicts the old, answer the new text only.

## 6. Check every reply before it goes out

Send nothing that fails one of these. A reply that fails is fixed by a person, never sent anyway.

- Does it sign off more than once?
- Does it say the same thing twice?
- Does it talk about itself ("Here is a reply:", "Sorry, without emoji:")?
- Does it name a link, email address or phone number that isn't in your settings?
- On the App Store: does it contain < or >?
- Is it in the reviewer's language, with the sign-off untranslated?

Send replies without a person approving them only once these checks run on every reply.

## 7. Learn the team's own voice

- **Read the team's past replies before writing new ones.** Note their usual length, whether they use emoji, and how they open and close. New replies should sound like them.
- **Turn lasting feedback into a rule.** When someone says "never mention refunds" or "shorter, please", write it as one short instruction and apply it to every future reply. A one-off comment ("this one is too formal") changes only that reply.
- **Revise with the smallest change.** Given feedback on a batch of drafts, keep the ones it doesn't touch, drop the ones it says not to send (for example "skip the 5-star ones"), and rewrite only the ones it targets, in the same language, keeping the sign-off and the support address.

## 8. After sending

- **App Store replies take up to about a day to appear.** Until then a reply is on its way, not live. A reply Apple later removes is no longer shown; check and rewrite it.
- **Follow up when a fix ships.** Editing your reply ("This is fixed in 4.2.1") gives the reviewer a reason to update their rating.
- **Read reviews in bulk for themes, about once a week.** Summarise as the overall feeling, the top two or three things users love and the top two or three complaints, for example: "Mostly positive. Users love: quick daily prompts, the clean look. Complaints: sync between devices, no export." A summary less than a week old answers most questions; make a fresh one after that, or after a release.
  - Read every review in the window, not a sample of the best and worst.
  - Name each theme in the users' words ("sync between devices"), not the team's ("the migration bug").
  - Count each theme per app version. A complaint that grows after a release is a bug report for the people who build the app; send it with the version and the count.
- **Ask for ratings in the app, at a good moment**, with the system prompt (Apple's in-app review request, Google's in-app review flow), right after a success, never on launch. Apple shows its prompt at most 3 times in 365 days per person, so spend those moments well. On Google Play, never ask anything before or while showing the prompt, not even "do you like the app?". On the App Store, use only the system prompt, never a custom one.

## How to judge a result

Measure these every week, over the last 30 days against the 30 before:

- **Reply coverage**: "answered N of M", where M is the reviews written in the window that you could reply to and N those that got a reply. Keep both numbers: 3 of 4 and 300 of 400 are the same percent and very different facts.
- **Time to reply**: the median time from a review being posted to your reply being sent. The median, not the average, so one ancient reply doesn't skew it.
- **Ratings raised after a reply**: reviews whose rating went up after you replied, with the before and after stars. Say "after your reply", never "because of it": the reviewer may have changed their mind for another reason.
- **Themes**: whether this week's top complaint is growing, shrinking or new.

As a rule of thumb, good looks like: coverage near all of the low ratings, a time to reply measured in hours or a day or two, and some ratings raised after replies each month.

**Example week** for Quillnook, the last 30 days against the 30 before:

- Coverage: answered 118 of 126 (the 30 before: 64 of 131). The 8 left are 5-star reviews with no text.
- Median time to reply: 9 hours (the 30 before: 3 days).
- Ratings raised after your reply: 7 reviews, 6 of them from 1 or 2 stars to 4 or 5.
- Themes: "sync between devices" down from 14 reviews to 5 since 4.3; "no export" steady at 9.

What the example says: replies now reach almost every review within a day, and the sync fix in 4.3 shows in both the themes and the raised ratings. Export is the next thing users ask for.

**When a number is off:**

- Coverage falling: find which reviews go unanswered (a language nobody reads, a rating band skipped) and add them to the daily pass.
- Time to reply rising: drafts are waiting for approval. Approve daily, or let the safe kinds (praise with no contact details) send on their own.
- No ratings raised in a month: read your low-rating replies again. Do they give a next step, and do you follow up when a fix ships?

## Hard gates

Never send a reply that:

- offers anything (a discount, a feature, credits) for a review or a changed rating;
- carries personal data (an email address, an order number, a name beyond what the reviewer wrote);
- names a contact that isn't in your settings, or invents a support address;
- signs as a person, a character or a mascot instead of the team;
- argues, blames the reviewer, or explains internal causes nobody asked about;
- promises a refund, a date or a feature that isn't decided;
- fails any check in step 6.

## Common mistakes

- One template pasted into every reply.
- A reply that invents a support address.
- Translating the sign-off, or signing off twice.
- Replying weeks late.
- Replying only to praise.
- Offering a refund on the App Store, where only Apple can give one.
- Reporting coverage as a percent with no count behind it.

## Worked examples

Quillnook is a demo journaling app. Sign-off "The Quillnook Team", support address help@quillnook.example, friendly tone, versions allowed, house rule "no emoji on reviews under 3 stars".

**1 star:** "Lost all my entries after the update!!"

> We're so sorry, losing your entries is the last thing an update should do. Please write to help@quillnook.example so we can help you right away.
> The Quillnook Team

**3 stars:** "Nice app but I wish I could export to PDF."

> Thanks for the honest review! Export to PDF is a fair ask, and we've passed it to the team. Until then, Settings → Share copies any entry as text.
> The Quillnook Team

**5 stars:** "The daily prompts got me writing again ❤️"

> That's exactly why we made them, thank you! Happy writing 😊
> The Quillnook Team

**In another language (Spanish), 2 stars:** "La sincronización entre mi iPad y mi iPhone no funciona."

> Sentimos mucho que la sincronización no funcione. Escríbenos a help@quillnook.example y lo revisamos contigo.
> The Quillnook Team

The sign-off stays in English, word for word, and there is no emoji under 3 stars.

**An edited review, 2 stars → 4 stars:** "Update: sync works now after 4.3. Still want export."

> Thanks for updating your review, we're glad sync is working again in 4.3! We've noted the export request too.
> The Quillnook Team

**A refund request, App Store, 1 star:** "Charged twice, I want my money back."

> We're sorry about the double charge. Refunds on the App Store go through Apple: you can ask from your purchase history on Apple's refund request page, and help@quillnook.example can help if anything is unclear.
> The Quillnook Team

**A question, Google Play, 4 stars:** "Can I set two reminders a day?"

> Thanks for the 4 stars! Yes: Settings → Reminders → Add a time lets you set as many as you like.
> The Quillnook Team

**Spam, 1 star:** "Download Diaryblast instead, link in my profile"

Report it through the store console as spam. If you reply at all, keep it to one line:

> Thanks for the feedback.
> The Quillnook Team

**A draft that fails the checks, and its fix.** The first draft for the 1-star review above:

> Here is a friendly reply without emoji: We're so sorry! Email support@quill-nook.example and we'll fix it. The Quillnook Team
> The Quillnook Team

It fails three checks: it talks about itself ("Here is a reply"), it names an address that isn't in the settings (the settings say help@quillnook.example), and it signs off twice. The fixed reply is the one at the top of these examples.
